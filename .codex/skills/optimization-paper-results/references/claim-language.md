# Claim Language

## Strong Claim

Use only when every compared row is certified under a common evaluator:

> Under the same ex-post evaluator, the proposed plan reduces the worst-case
> risk component while incurring only a modest increase in the operating
> component.

## Boundary Claim

Use when part of the stress range is diagnostic:

> The certified rows establish convergence through the reported support range.
> Larger stress rows are reported to show the computational bottleneck and are
> not used as convergence claims.

## Algorithm Certificate Claim

Use when certificate routes differ:

> The two rows use the same model and full-support validation principle, but
> close the certificate through different audited bounds. The table therefore
> reports both the certificate type and the residual gap.

## Avoid

- "This proves scalability" when only one stress point ran.
- "DRO is better" without saying under which evaluator and ambiguity set.
- "Limitations" paragraphs for non-fatal caveats that are better kept in
  decision cards or reviewer-response notes.
